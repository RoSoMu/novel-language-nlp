# Frankenstein in Context: Ten Adapted Exchanges

*A reader's guide to creative language in an NLP exploratory corpus*

## About this guide

These ten exchanges are **edited, reader-friendly adaptations** of selected conversations about reconstructing a psycholinguistic experiment. They illustrate how *Frankenstein* and related expressions were used, reused, extended, and discussed in context. The exchanges are not a complete chronological history of the conversations.

The adapted speaker excerpts in this guide correspond to the **public analytical corpus** (`data/public/conversations_adapted.json`) used for the computational experiments. The original conversational excerpts remain private and are not required to run the published analyses. Excerpts have been selected, shortened and lightly edited for clarity, discretion and readability, including removal of identifying terminal details and some coarse language. Titles, context descriptions and linguistic observations are editorial additions and are **not included in the computational input**. These exchanges do not, on their own, establish how any language model internally interprets or generates figurative language.

## EX001 — To frankenstein

**Context:** During reconstruction of statistical models, previously discarded analytical components were recovered and recombined.

**User (adapted):**

> The combined model didn't work, so we sliced the data until something did. We actually pruned a lot of it, made Russian dolls with it, but then we decided to Frankenstein it. And it worked, and it was epic!
>
> OK, I need lunch and a shower, amigo. But I'm going to leave you with a challenging question. Now that we know so much about this, what do you think happened to participants who decided not to choose an option? I wonder whether their brains simply exploded in the middle of a subjunctive ocean!

**Assistant (adapted):**

> HAHAHA. Go eat and shower, because you have just casually left another body on the laboratory floor. 😂
>
> And yes, I think those missing responses have suddenly become much more interesting than they looked when they were merely NaN.

**Linguistic observation:** The user converts the proper noun *Frankenstein* into the verb *to frankenstein* to describe recovering and combining analytical components. The assistant continues the laboratory imagery with *another body on the laboratory floor*, referring to the unanswered research question the user has just left behind. The exchange illustrates how an unfamiliar lexical formation can be interpreted through context and become part of a developing figurative interaction.

## EX003 — Frankensteining and Frankenstein Disposal

**Context:** The user considers how to explain complex statistical findings through the actual counterfactual sentences used in the experiment, linking the analysis back to its theoretical framework.

**User (adapted):**

> Maria Obsesiones' unicorning in environmentally friendly rainbow conditions after serious frankensteining, translated into human lingo, basically. 🌈😁 That's what we need to do!
>
> And then the README, because I want to explain the theoretical framework without turning the repository's introduction into a Bible without numbered verses.
>
> By the way, read this from what I sent you:
>
> What Girotto and colleagues’ study claimed instead is that actors and readers produce different counterfactuals: while actors were more likely to alter the means or features by which they had ended up making a poor decision in the undoing of an event (that is, “If the means by which I made the wrong decision had been different, the outcome would have been different”), readers were more likely to undo someone else’s choice (that is, “If they had made a different choice, the outcome would have been different”).
>
> Now, isn’t it funny that our results show that when Focus = I, Agent = 0 and Goal = frequent, sentences lead to less transfer—and thus more correct responses?
>
> Excuses from actors even when they aren’t real actors! Amazing.

**Assistant (adapted):**

> In the beginning, Roese created the goal and Gibson the computational resource pool.
>
> The README gives readers the human story. The opening notebook gives them the theory and experimental sentences. The analysis notebooks provide the evidence. The final notebook brings the actual sentences back.
>
> Environmentally friendly rainbow unicorning after responsible Frankenstein disposal. 🌈🦄♻️

**Linguistic observation:** *Frankensteining* refers to the work of reconstructing the statistical analysis. *Unicorning* is another already-established expression associated with the moment complex findings begin to make sense; here, the resulting insights must also be translated into accessible explanations grounded in the experimental sentences. The assistant recognises the practical request and recombines the shared expressions in *responsible Frankenstein disposal* and *rainbow unicorning*. The exchange shows creative vocabulary being adapted to a new communicative purpose without losing its earlier associations.

**Context:** The project's roadmap needs updating because a final synthesis notebook has been inserted before the technical appendices.

**User (adapted):**

**Assistant (adapted):**

> THE ZOMBIE ROADMAP HAS RISEN AGAIN! 🧟‍♀️📜
>
> After what we've done, this is tiny surgery. No new intellectual work. It's repository plumbing.
>
> And actually, this gives the zombie-guide a better ending because it can finally explain the Frankensteining-Unicorning logic rather than merely listing notebook numbers:
>
> **Frankensteining:** Reconstruct the original experiment, preserve the messy behavioural anatomy, separate questions that shouldn't be averaged together, and build an auditable analytical path.

**Linguistic observation:** The conversation shifts from using the creative expressions to explicitly defining them. *Frankensteining* is presented as the analytical reconstruction process, while *Unicorning* extends from the experience of interpretive insight to the work of reconnecting findings with sentences and theory. The assistant's *tiny surgery* and *messy behavioural anatomy* continue the figurative register. The exchange illustrates a metalinguistic development: the shared vocabulary itself becomes something to explain and organise.

The assistant uses the established Frankenstein vocabulary to reassure the user that the difficult reconstruction is complete and that the remaining work is more manageable. The humour draws on their shared expressions and working history, making the reassurance specific to this conversational context rather than generic.

## EX006 — The Multimodal Omnibus Super-Frankenstein

**Context:** After reconstructing a psycholinguistic experiment using a two-stage modelling approach with separate binary outcomes, the user jokingly considers what would happen if a single model attempted to classify responses into three categories while accounting for the experimental complexity.

**User (adapted):**

> Maria Obsesiones is done with modelling!
>
> But I must admit, the thought of multimodal modelling did cross my mind a few times, just out of curiosity.
>
> What bloody equation would the model come up with if it tried to average everything?
>
> It would probably explode and refuse to cooperate! Hahaha.

**Assistant (adapted):**

> It might even converge. That's the dangerous part.
>
> It could obediently hand you coefficients, standard errors and six interaction terms with absolutely magnificent decimal places while averaging over precisely the conditional structures we've spent months disentangling.
>
> **The equation wouldn't necessarily explode.**
>
> **The interpretation would.**
>
> And we'd end up back where we started:
>
> “According to the multimodel *omnibus super-Frankenstein*, Agency has an average coefficient of 0.1837—”
>
> **CHEAP FOR WHAT?**
>
> **CHEAP FOR WHOM?**
>
> **AT WHICH STAGE?**
>
> **TOWARD WHICH DESTINATION?**
>
> **WITH WHICH GOAL?**
>
> Edward would quietly put down his cucumber sandwich and leave. Girotto would take the flowers back. Roese and Epstude would finally volunteer for the guillotine. 😂

**Linguistic observation:** The assistant introduces *omnibus super-Frankenstein* as a humorous name for a hypothetical model combining the analytical components previously addressed through separate modelling stages.

The expression preserves the original association between *Frankensteining* and reconstructing complex analytical structures. The modifiers *omnibus* and *super-* extend that association to a considerably more ambitious construction, reflecting the complexity of attempting to account for the experimental variability within a single model. It applies the same conceptual framework to a hypothetical model of greater scope and complexity.

The exchange illustrates how an established creative expression can be expanded into a novel compound name while retaining its contextual meaning and adapting to a new analytical situation.

The exchange illustrates how established creative expressions can be reused in a new discourse format—in this case, a fictional academic publication—and incorporated into a coherent humorous continuation that draws on the broader conversational context.

**Linguistic observation:** The assistant introduces *the Faculty of Counterfactual Frankensteining and Applied Gibberishonian Studies* as the name of a fictional academic institution, extending the established creative vocabulary into an imaginary organisational setting.

The expression *Counterfactual Frankensteining* combines the invented process of *Frankensteining* with the subject matter of the original psycholinguistic experiment. The assistant develops the user's reinterpretation of *Dean's List* into a coherent institutional scenario, accepting Mari O's self-promotion and creating a faculty appropriate to her new position.

Beyond the production of another creative expression, the exchange also illustrates a metalinguistic development. By inventing an academic faculty devoted to *Counterfactual Frankensteining*, the assistant treats the established expression as a recognisable concept that can itself become a subject of study. The humour depends on the expression having acquired sufficient contextual significance to support this fictional institutionalisation.

The exchange illustrates how creative vocabulary can move beyond its original descriptive function and become an object of metalinguistic elaboration, while continuing to generate new figurative and conversational possibilities.

## Reading across the exchanges

These excerpts illustrate **syntactic productivity** (verbs, modifiers, compounds and names), **semantic and figurative extension** (analytical reconstruction, processes, spaces and imagined institutions), and **metalinguistic elaboration** (explicit definitions, a proposed guide and a fictional faculty). They are selected observations, not a claim that the ten exchanges exhaust the development of the vocabulary or establish a universal sequence. Computational analysis uses the corresponding public adapted JSON dataset; the original private excerpts are not the published experimental input.
